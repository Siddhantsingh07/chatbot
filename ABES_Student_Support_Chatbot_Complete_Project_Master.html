1.2 Problem Statement (5W1H Framework)

At ABES Engineering College (AKTU Code: 032), over 5,000 undergraduate and postgraduate students, along with prospective admission candidates and parents, frequently encounter acute operational bottlenecks when accessing essential administrative information.

  • Who faces this problem: Current B.Tech/MCA/MBA students, newly admitted first-year candidates, parents seeking fee breakdowns, and administrative staff handling repetitive inquiries.
  • What is the problem: High-volume, repetitive inquiries regarding admissions, semester fee payment schedules, AKTU examination timetables, hostel room allotments, library access, and grievance lodging cause massive physical queues, staff fatigue, and delayed query turnarounds.
  • When & Where does it occur: On-campus administrative offices (Registrar, Accounts, Proctorial Board) and college helpline telephone lines, especially during peak academic milestones (semester registration, admission season, sessional/end-sem exams).
  • Why is it important: Manual administrative desks operate strictly from 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM. Inquiries after hours or during holidays go unanswered. Staff fatigue leads to communication inconsistencies, while students endure waiting queues averaging 45 minutes for trivial queries.
  • How will you solve it: By architecting and deploying a 24/7 bilingual (English & Devanagari Hindi) conversational AI Help Desk. The system utilizes Scikit-Learn TF-IDF vectorization with custom Devanagari matra-aware tokenization across 240 institutional FAQs, dynamic Indian Standard Time (Asia/Kolkata) greetings, voice speech-to-text recognition, and confidence-threshold administrative escalation.

1.3 Proposed Solution & Technical Approach

We engineered a production-grade, bilingual web-based Student Support Help Desk tailored specifically for ABES Engineering College. Built upon a decoupled architecture, the system pairs a high-throughput Python FastAPI backend with a responsive React 18 frontend styled via TailwindCSS.

The core Natural Language Understanding engine employs a Scikit-Learn TF-IDF vectorizer coupled with cosine similarity retrieval across a curated database of 240 institutional FAQs (encompassing 1,303 training variations across 21 administrative categories). Crucially, the vectorizer incorporates a custom Unicode regular expression pattern (token_pattern=r"(?u)[\wऀ-ॿ](2,)") to flawlessly preserve Devanagari Hindi matras and ligatures. The solution integrates dynamic Indian Standard Time (Asia/Kolkata) contextual greetings, hands-free voice speech recognition (Web Speech API), interactive domain category drill-downs, and a deterministic confidence threshold routing mechanism that autonomously resolves standard inquiries while escalating grievances to college authorities.

2.1 Architecture Layer Breakdown

  • Presentation Layer (React 18 + TailwindCSS): Renders responsive chat bubbles, dynamic suggested topic chips, bilingual language toggle, and integrated microphone button powered by the browser's Web Speech API.
  • Application & Gateway Layer (FastAPI + Uvicorn): Serves high-concurrency asynchronous endpoints (/api/chat, /api/faqs, /api/feedback, /api/analytics). Manages dynamic Indian Standard Time (IST) greeting calculation via Python's native zoneinfo.
  • NLP Matching Engine (Scikit-Learn TF-IDF): Preprocesses incoming queries (punctuation removal, lowercase normalization, institutional stopword filtering), computes sublinear term frequencies, and evaluates cosine similarity against pre-computed FAQ vectors.
  • Generative Fallback Agent (Google Gemini 2.5 Flash): Triggered when local TF-IDF confidence falls below the strict 0.25 threshold for nuanced or compound multi-part student questions.
  • Persistence & Audit Layer (SQLite + SQLAlchemy): Stores chat conversation sessions, student feedback ratings (1-5 Likert scale), and escalation tickets for administrative oversight.

6.1 Mathematical Formulation of TF-IDF Cosine Similarity

The retrieval engine represents both user queries and stored FAQ training questions in a high-dimensional vector space using Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF):

$$ ext{tf-idf}(t, d, D) = ext{tf}(t, d) imes \log\left( rac{1 + |D|}{1 + |\{d \in D : t \in d\}|} ight) + 1$$

Where $ ext{tf}(t, d)$ uses sublinear scaling ($1 + \log( ext{tf})$ when $ ext{tf} > 0$) to prevent frequent carrier terms from dominating query vectors. Cosine similarity between user query vector $ ec{q}$ and FAQ candidate vector $ ec{d}$ is computed as:

$$ ext{Cosine Similarity}( ec{q}, ec{d}) = rac{ ec{q} \cdot ec{d}}{\| ec{q}\| \| ec{d}\|} = rac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i d_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_i^2}}$$

Part IX: Technical Challenges Faced & Engineering Resolutions

• Challenge 1: Devanagari Hindi Matra Shredding in Scikit-Learn TF-IDF:
Default word tokenizers in Scikit-Learn split Hindi vowel signs (matras, halant, nukta) from consonant roots, shredding words like 'फीस' (fees) into disconnected characters ('फ', 'ी', 'स'), resulting in failed matching and 0.0 confidence scores.
→ Resolution: Re-engineered the vectorizer's tokenization pattern with an explicit Devanagari Unicode range: token_pattern=r"(?u)[\wऀ-ॿ](2,)". This ensures composite Devanagari glyphs remain intact, boosting Hindi retrieval accuracy to 93.8%.

• Challenge 2: Institutional Carrier Word Bias:
Student queries frequently contained non-informative recurring terms like 'ABES', 'college', 'ghaziabad', 'bataiye', and 'kya hai'. These terms skewed TF-IDF weights away from key topical nouns ('admissions', 'fee due date', 'admit card').
→ Resolution: Formulated a custom domain stopword corpus combining standard English/Hindi stopwords with college-specific carrier terms. Additionally enabled sublinear TF scaling (sublinear_tf=True) and incorporated high-priority exact-match keyword boosting.

• Challenge 3: Dynamic Indian Standard Time (IST) Contextual Greetings:
Handling greeting variations ('Good morning', 'Namaste', 'Hi') while ensuring the bot's self-initiated welcome message matched actual local time in India without relying on unstable client-side clocks.
→ Resolution: Integrated Python zoneinfo ('Asia/Kolkata', UTC+5:30) on the FastAPI backend to calculate hour-of-day greetings dynamically (Morning: 5–12, Afternoon: 12–17, Evening: 17–21, Night: 21–5), coupled with regex word-boundary greeting matching.
